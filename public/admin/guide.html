Tuesday Night Arguing

How to write here

Everything you need, on one page. Nothing you write can break the site, and nothing you write goes public until you say so.

Getting in

  1. Open the email from Netlify and click Accept the invite.
  2. Choose a password. That is the whole sign-up.
  3. After that, the way back in is tuesday-night-arguing.netlify.app/admin. Bookmark it.

There is no software to install and no account to make anywhere else.

What a chapter is made of

A chapter is a stack of blocks. You add them, delete them, and drag them around. There are four kinds, and the first two are the whole idea of the book.

Noct's telling

You, afterwards, telling the story. Write it like you'd write anything — bold and italic are buttons, there is no code to learn.

Interruption — the room

The five of us, in the middle of it. This is what one looks like on the page:

Torgan 21:47
single. always single.

Merlin
that's a kitchen. you want double.

moved Noct to Room 4

You type it one line per line:

  • **Torgan** 21:47 single. always single. — a name in double asterisks, then the time if you know it, then what was said. The time is optional.
  • *moved Noct to Room 4* — single asterisks either side for something the app said rather than a person.

Don't worry about the exact times. If you remember it was late, say 23:10 and move on. The point is the texture, not the log.

Read-aloud

Something heard rather than seen — a voice, a vision, words off a wall. It gets a box of its own, the way a module does.

Table

A period rulebook table. Fiddlier than the rest. If you want one, write the rows however you like and let Pete format it.

Interrupting something you've already written

This is the bit worth knowing. You've written a paragraph, and halfway through it you realise that's exactly where somebody asked about radiators.

Don't cut and paste. Put the cursor where the break should fall, open the + menu in the prose toolbar, and choose Split here — start a new block. Pick what goes in the gap, then save.

The paragraph becomes two, with your new empty block sitting between them, in exactly the right place. Click it and write.

You'll see a marked divider where the break will happen before you save. It disappears once the split is done — it never appears on the real page.

Starting a new chapter

+ Chapter, at the top of the chapter list. Give it a title and a number — the number is what orders the book, so a chapter between 3 and 4 is a problem to tell Pete about, not to solve alone.

Nothing goes public by accident

Every new chapter starts with Published switched off. It exists, it saves, Pete can read it, and nobody else can see it. When it is ready, switch it on.

Save is the Publish button at the top — confusingly named, and it only means "write this down". The Published switch is the one that decides whether readers see it.

If something goes wrong

  • Nothing you do is destructive. Every save is kept forever, so anything deleted can be brought back.
  • A save that fails leaves the site exactly as it was. You cannot take the book down by writing in it.
  • The right-hand pane is a close approximation, not a photograph of the finished page. Small differences in spacing are the preview, not your writing.
  • If the editor won't load, refresh once, then tell Pete.

One ask

Write the interruptions as badly as they actually happened. The temptation will be to tidy them into jokes. The funny thing isn't the line, it's that four grown men stopped a dragon fight to cost a central heating job, and nobody thought that was strange at the time.
