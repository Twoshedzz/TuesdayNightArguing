    text: |-
      The stairs let us into a room with runes on every surface and a fountain at
      the north end. Four braziers kept it at the exact level of light where you
      can see that there is something to see and not what it is. There was a rune
      painted large across the middle of the floor, and paper everywhere — notes,
      pages, somebody's working. I went round quietly and put the ones that looked
      like arcana into my coat, and I am not going to pretend that was for the
      group.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      An orc and a thug came through the battered south door shouting that this was
      theirs. It was a hard fight and a close one, and it ended with Torgan putting
      his second swing exactly where his first had been, and the room going quiet
      all at once.

      We took the orc's ears. Five gold. I have already told you about the bounty.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      There was a gnome in the alcove.

      He had been in there for some time, by the state of him, and he was so
      frightened that he could not get a sentence out in any of the four languages
      he tried. I put a dagger on him, because that is what you do with something
      found in an alcove in a ruin, and asked who he was.

      His name was Garnel.

      I want to be clear that there was a long moment in that room where it could
      have gone the other way, and that the reason it did not is not that I am a
      good man. Somewhere in the next few minutes I put the dagger away and took
      his hand instead, and we stood there holding hands like children at a river
      crossing, and he came with us. I could not tell you now whether I did that to
      reassure him or to keep hold of him.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      The witch we never saw at all.

      You could tell where she was by the steam. It moved around something, the way
      water goes round a stone, and that was the whole of our targeting information
      for the entire engagement. Garnel, who had been a hostage for about an hour,
      turned out to be the most useful person in the room.

      We took four stones out of that place. Old, blue, heavier than they look. Four
      down and a great many to go, and a gnome following us about at the back and
      flinching at noises, and nobody had yet said out loud that he was coming with
      us. He simply was, and he still is, and he has saved every one of our lives
      more times than the four of us put together.
