      Garnel could read it. That is the whole reason any of this happened, and it
      is worth saying plainly, because a week earlier he had been a shape in an
      alcove that I nearly killed.

      There was a pool in the deep room, and there were marks around it, and the
      marks were a ritual rather than a decoration. He went round it twice, muttering,
      and then told us what it did, and then told us he was fairly sure, and then
      told us he was reasonably sure, and we decided that was good enough because
      we are not serious people.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Torgan and I stood in the water.

      I will not be able to describe this properly. It was not warm. Nothing
      glowed. There was no moment. We stood in it for a while feeling faintly
      ridiculous, and then we got out, and I was stronger than I have ever been in
      my life, and so was he, and we both knew it before either of us said anything.

      Not stronger the way a potion makes you. Stronger the way you are stronger at
      thirty than at twenty — settled into it, as though it had always been the case
      and I had been doing it wrong until now.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Somebody in Fallcrest was paying fifty gold apiece for these stones and had
      not mentioned this.

      I have turned that over a great deal since. Either our employer did not know
      what they were, in which case he is a fool with money, or he did know, in
      which case we were sent sixty-two miles east by a man who wanted a sacrament
      dug up and carted back in a sack.

      I know which one I believe. I did not raise it at the time. We had stones to
      find and a mayor's letter in Torgan's coat, and that is the sort of thinking
      you do on the road home, when it is too late to be useful.
