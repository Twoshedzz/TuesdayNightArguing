summary: "First blood on the road, a nest that erupts out of the ground, and something I dug out of a grave."

  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Twenty miles the first day, which is twenty miles of other people's legs and
      rather more of mine. Wolves came at the watch that night. I will not pretend
      it was a battle. Four of them, and we were five, and we were frightened
      enough to be quick.

      That was the first blood. It did not feel like anything. I remember noticing
      that it did not feel like anything, and filing the observation away as a thing
      to be concerned about later, which is a habit I would recommend to nobody.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      The second evening we made camp on bad ground. You could smell that it was
      bad — a dry, sour smell coming up out of a scatter of holes in the earth, and
      we discussed it, and we stayed, because we had walked twenty miles again and
      the alternative was walking further.

      The ground came apart at dusk. Axebeaks are enormous and they are fast and
      they come up underneath you, and for about ninety seconds the camp was simply
      noise. I killed one. My first, properly mine, and I had the handaxe in both
      hands and no idea how it had got there.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Afterwards there was a hole where the nest had been, and under the nest a
      chamber, and in the chamber bones. A great many bones, and the sort of filth
      that takes centuries to accumulate, and me, crawling through it on my elbows,
      because somebody had to and I am the one who is shaped for tunnels.

      I came out with two things. A brooch, old, heavy, good work. And a drum.

      The skin of it had rotted away a very long time ago and the frame should have
      gone with it and had not. I cast detect magic on the brooch, because the
      brooch was obviously the valuable thing, and the brooch was nothing at all.
      The drum glowed. Faintly, steadily, like something breathing in its sleep.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      I have carried it ever since. It cannot be played. I have never got a note
      out of it and I have tried in private more often than I would admit in front
      of the others.

      That was the evening the job stopped being a job. We had been hired to fetch
      stones. Nobody had said anything about graves.
