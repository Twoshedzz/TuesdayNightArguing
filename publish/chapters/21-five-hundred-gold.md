      Tobs paid us five hundred gold.

      He counted it out himself in a back room, slowly, the way somebody does when
      the sum is larger than anything they have handled before and they are
      determined not to be the person who gets it wrong. The last time I had seen
      him he had been unconscious on a pub floor with my hands on his chest.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      The mayor wanted a conversation, which is never free.

      He wanted to know about the King of Fear, a name we had heard three times
      underground and never understood. He wanted to know about the Dragon, which
      we could tell him about only because a lizardfolk elder had mentioned it in
      passing as a fact of local weather. He would pay two hundred up front to find
      out more.

      Four separate pieces of work were put in front of us that afternoon. We took
      them all, because we were richer than we had ever been and that is apparently
      how it goes.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      We were better than we had been, measurably. There were twelve of us by the
      end of the week — hirelings, specialists, people whose names I had to write
      down to remember, where a season earlier there had been four strangers off a
      boat and an old dwarf who drank alone.

      And that is where I would stop it, if it were a story.

      Four people meet on a boat, go east after something nobody understands, come
      back changed and paid, and find the world rearranged behind their backs. There
      is a shape to that. It ends on the road with the refugees, with a bag of
      sacred stones that nobody wants, and with five people who have done one
      genuinely unforgivable thing between them and have agreed not to discuss it.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      It is not a story, though. It is what happened, and what happened is that we
      went back out again almost immediately, and everything after this gets
      considerably worse.
