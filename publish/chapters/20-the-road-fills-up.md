      The road filled up a day out from home.

      Not with traffic. With people. Carts, and people walking beside carts, and
      people walking without carts carrying what they had been able to pick up,
      coming the other way in a steady, unhurried, terrible stream that went on for
      hours.

      You do not ask the front of a column like that what has happened. You wait
      until somebody tells you, because the front of it is the people who are still
      in shock and the middle is the people who have started talking.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      What we were told, over and over, in slightly different words by a great many
      exhausted strangers, was this.

      The city is gone. There is a rift. There is a portal, and it is in the city,
      and the city has been evacuated — all of it, everyone, out.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      I had a bag over my shoulder with old blue stones in it.

      We had been underground for weeks. We had been strengthened in a holy pool and
      nearly killed by a dwarf who grew, and run away from a city of our own, and
      we had fought a shaman in a hall where the people who died were the ones we
      had freed that morning. All of that had been, to us, enormous.

      And while it was happening, somewhere behind us, the actual world had done
      something so much larger that nobody on that road had heard of Ethium, or of
      the stones, or of us.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Nobody said anything for a long way.

      I remember Garnel walking next to Magnus with his hands behind his back, and
      Torgan carrying more of the weight than his share without being asked, and
      Shae up ahead where he goes when he does not want to be looked at.

      We had gone out to fetch something and come back to find the shelf gone.
