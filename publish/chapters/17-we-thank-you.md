  - type: telling
    text: |-
      It was over a mile of climbing to get out, and most of it was climbing rather
      than walking, and when the air changed none of us said anything in case we
      were wrong.

      Daylight. After that long underground it is not a relief, it is an assault.
      We came out into a swamp with the sun on it and stood blinking like things
      turned out of a cupboard.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      The village was built on walkways over the water. Lizardfolk, a great many of
      them, and Kor and Mimi going ahead of us talking fast.

      Their elder came out to meet us. Chozzo. He greeted us in Draconic, which
      none of us has, and then — having established that — he did something I have
      thought about ever since. He switched to Common. He does not have Common. He
      had enough of it to be courteous and not one word more, and he used all of it
      on us, slowly, one word at a time:

      *We. Thank. You.*
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      We showed him the stones.

      I had been carrying them for weeks by then. We had dug for them and been
      hunted for them and nearly died for them twice, and I put them out on the
      boards in front of him with, I am embarrassed to say, a certain amount of
      ceremony.

      He looked at them and said they did not concern his people, and moved on.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      He gave us gauntlets and a set of pan pipes, which I have, and which are
      better than anything I had.

      And then he told us about the Dragon. Not a dragon. The Dragon — the thing
      that takes from them and that they do not fight, named the way you name
      weather. He did not dress it up and he did not ask us to do anything about
      it. He simply told us it was there, in the tone of a man explaining where the
      river floods.

      We left the next morning with a bag of stones nobody wanted and one new piece
      of information that nobody had asked for.
