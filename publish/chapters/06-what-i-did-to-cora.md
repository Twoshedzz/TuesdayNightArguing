    text: |-
      Her name was Cora and we tied her to a post.

      I asked her questions first. That is the part I notice myself wanting to put
      at the front, so let me be honest about why: because it makes what follows
      look like a last resort, and it was not a last resort. It was the second
      thing I tried.

      She would not say who had sent them or what they were doing in the tower, and
      I ran out of patience in about the time it takes to ask twice.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      There was a fire going. I put her face in it.

      She screamed and she talked and I did not like the answer, so I did it again.
      She talked more. I asked her about the Blue Moon, about the five men who came
      through the door that night, and I did not believe her, and I did it a third
      time.

      I am not going to dress this up. I had a fire and a tied woman and a
      question, and I used the first on the second to get the third, and it worked,
      which is the detail that keeps me awake. She told us Bram had been carrying a
      bag of holding. We would not have found it otherwise.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      The others were appalled.

      Magnus would not look at me. Magnus, who had taken a man's jaw off with a
      maul an hour earlier, could not be in the same half of the camp as me, and I
      understood the distinction perfectly and resented it anyway.

      And I defended myself. That is the part I am least proud of, more than the
      fire. I stood in front of four people and listed my injuries like a bill. I
      had fought twice that day. I had walked ten miles on legs that are shorter
      than theirs and I walk them further than any of them. I had taken two arrows.
      I had crawled through a waterfall on my hands and knees in front of all of
      them.

      Not one word of that is an argument. Every word of it is true, and I meant
      it, and I would say it again.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      Shae ended it. He offered to walk her to the treeline and let her go, and
      Magnus said he would be going too, and Shae agreed, pleasantly, and then —
      only to me, only for a moment — he winked.

      I have never asked him what happened at the treeline. Magnus came back and
      said she had gone. I have decided to believe him and I have decided not to
      ask Shae, and those are two different decisions, and I made them both on
      purpose.
  - type: telling
    text: |-
      We slept on it. We were better the next morning in the way that people are
      better at things, which is to say stronger, steadier, more capable of this
      sort of work than we had been the day before.

      The group was changed. They were careful around me after that, and polite,
      and a little slower to agree with me, and nothing was ever said about it
      again by anybody including me until now.
